# Call prep: Northbridge Landscaping & Snow

**Domain:** northbridgelandscape.example  
**Fit score:** 55/100

## Why this score

- Seasonal workforce, from about 40 year-round to roughly 140 at peak, which is a strong fit for seasonal payroll and onboarding.
- Landscaping is a target industry and Massachusetts is the home territory.
- H-2B use adds compliance complexity.
- Single state and small size limit the score, and no open HR or payroll roles were observed.

## Company snapshot (sourced facts)

- Serves residential and commercial customers in Hampshire and Franklin counties, Massachusetts; lawn and landscape care from April through November, snow removal from December through March. [1] _(confidence: high)_
- Serving the Valley since 1998. About 40 year-round staff, growing to roughly 140 people in peak season, from April through October. [2] _(confidence: high)_
- Some seasonal workers join through the H-2B visa program. Only yard and office is in Hadley, MA. [2] _(confidence: high)_
- Careers page: hiring seasonal crew members and crew leaders for the April start, paid weekly, overtime available during peak season; snow plow operators also needed for winter. [3] _(confidence: high)_

## Pain-point hypotheses (inferences, not facts)

- **Ramping from about 40 to roughly 140 staff each season creates heavy onboarding, I-9/new-hire reporting and offboarding workload.** _(confidence: high)_ [2][3]
  - Why: About page states the headcount swing; careers page shows an April seasonal hiring push.
- **Weekly pay with peak-season overtime likely makes overtime calculation and timekeeping accuracy a pain point.** _(confidence: medium)_ [3]
  - Why: Careers page says paid weekly with overtime available during peak season. Time-tracking tools are unknown.
- **H-2B workers add compliance and record-keeping requirements (prevailing wage, documentation) that a small team may find burdensome.** _(confidence: medium)_ [2]
  - Why: About page mentions H-2B use. The size of the H-2B program and who handles compliance are unknown.
- **Two-season operation (landscape and snow) may mean mixed job roles, with workers moving between roles and pay rates, and layoffs or rehires at season changes.** _(confidence: low)_ [1]
  - Why: Home page shows landscape April-November and snow December-March; the exact transition handling is not documented.

## Discovery questions

1. How do you onboard the spring seasonal ramp-up today, and how long does it take to get a crew member paid?
2. How do you track hours and calculate overtime across crews and job sites?
3. How do you handle H-2B paperwork and wage requirements, and who owns it?
4. What happens to payroll and benefits for staff moving between landscape and snow seasons?
5. Who handles payroll and HR now, and is it a pain point at peak headcount?
6. Do you rehire returning seasonal workers, and how do you manage their records?

## Likely objections

- **We're small and our current payroll process works well enough.**
  - Response: Acknowledge that. Then point to the swing from about 40 to roughly 140 people, and ask how the spring onboarding rush goes with the current process.
- **Our workforce is seasonal, so we don't need a year-round HR service.**
  - Response: Ask whether the service can scale with headcount through the season. Seasonal onboarding, overtime and H-2B record-keeping are where a service helps most.
- **We already use a bookkeeper or accountant for payroll.**
  - Response: Offer to complement them. Position the service as handling onboarding, time and overtime, and compliance, so they can focus on the books.
